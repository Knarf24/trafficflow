# TrafficFlow

**Live app: https://knarf24.github.io/trafficflow/**

TrafficFlow is for vehicle queues on business property: drive-throughs, parking-lot exits and pickup lanes. You can simulate the queue car by car, forecast arrivals with machine learning, plan staff for each shift and count cars from a video. All of the Python runs in your browser with [Pyodide](https://pyodide.org/), so nothing is uploaded.

> **The forecasting data is synthetic.** The 14 weeks of drive-through arrivals were generated by
> [`training/make_data.py`](training/make_data.py) from a documented, made-up process. The scores below show
> that the method works on held-out data. They say nothing about accuracy at a real restaurant.

## What it does

| Tab | What happens | Python |
|---|---|---|
| **Simulate** | Compare a current and a proposed setup on exactly the same random customers, repeated 10–60 times. Watch one run car by car. | `trafficflow/sim.py` |
| **Forecast (ML)** | Linear regression, random forest and gradient boosting are scored against simple guesses on weeks they never saw. Includes a low–high range, what drives the forecast, error by period, a next-15-minutes what-if and a new day with live weather from Open-Meteo. | `trafficflow/forecast.py`, `training/train.py` |
| **Staff planner** | Picks order takers and kitchen crew for each shift by simulating every allowed option against the forecast. Tested on 14 held-out days. | `trafficflow/planner.py` |
| **Car counter** | A pretrained NanoDet-Plus detector finds vehicles in a video or photo. Each car is tracked and counted once when it crosses a line. Counts can become the arrival rate, the road traffic past the exit, the forecast's "last 15 minutes" or the cars already in line. | `trafficflow/vision.py` |

### Car-by-car simulation (discrete-event)

- Every car gets its own arrival time from a Poisson process whose rate changes every 15 minutes. Each car also gets its own random order, preparation, hand-off and merge times.
- Stages have servers and a **fixed number of waiting spaces**. A car that finishes but has no room ahead stays put and **blocks** its spot. So a full pickup lane stops the order point, and a busy road stops the window.
- Drive-through: order point → pickup window → exit onto the road. The kitchen crew prepares orders in parallel, and a car waits at the window for its food. When the stack is full, a car either drives away or **waits on the public street**.
- Parking exit: exit aisle → pay gate → exit onto the road. Cars that can't get into the aisle wait in their parking space.
- Leaving needs a safe gap in road traffic. Road traffic in vehicles per hour becomes the mean wait for a gap through Adams' gap-acceptance formula.
- The simulation reports each car's real time on site (average and the slowest 5%), cars lost, car-minutes on the street, the peak queue and the time to clear. Cars still stuck when the run stops are counted, never dropped.
- Staffing can change during the day (a schedule).

### Forecast results (test weeks 13–14, never used for training or choosing)

The task is to predict cars arriving per 15-minute slot. About 8.2 cars arrive per slot on average.

| Model | Tomorrow (day-ahead) MAE | Next 15 minutes MAE |
|---|---|---|
| Gradient boosting | 2.72 | 2.80 |
| **Random forest** (chosen on validation weeks) | **2.76** | **2.80** |
| Linear regression (Ridge) | 2.84 | 2.90 |
| Simple guess: average of the last 4 weeks | 2.98 | – |
| Simple guess: same time last week | 3.70 | 3.70 |
| Simple guess: same as the last 15 minutes | – | 3.75 |

- The random forest was chosen on the validation weeks. On the test weeks, gradient boosting turned out slightly better. Both are reported as they came out.
- Day-ahead: 7.6% less error than the best simple guess. Next 15 minutes: 24% less.
- The 10–90% range (quantile gradient boosting) held the real count 78% (day-ahead) and 77% (next 15 minutes) of the time, against an aim of 80%.
- Permutation importance shows that the day-ahead model mostly leans on the 4-week average for the same slot.

### Does planning from the forecast help? (14 held-out days)

Each plan is scored on the day's *actual* arrivals with fresh random seeds. These are averages per day.

| Plan | Staff-hours | Avg time on site | Cars lost | Total cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed rota, 1 order taker + 4 crew all day | 85.0 | 3.81 min | 3.2 | $2,021 |
| Planned from last week's counts | 50.0 | 4.40 min | 5.0 | $1,573 |
| Planned from the ML forecast | 45.4 | 4.78 min | 7.2 | $1,578 |
| Planned with hindsight (best case) | 50.9 | 4.00 min | 0.6 | $1,484 |

Planning from any forecast cuts the modelled cost by about 22% compared with a flat rota. The ML forecast's better accuracy has **not** yet turned into cheaper staffing than planning from last week's counts: it was cheaper on 6 of 14 days, and $5 per day more expensive on average. Hindsight shows there is about $90 per day still to gain. Cost assumptions: $16/hour wage, $0.30 per minute of customer waiting, $6 per lost car, $1 per car-minute on the street. All of these can be changed in the app.

## Honest limits

- The forecasting data is synthetic. Service times and site sizes are assumptions you can change, not measurements. Nothing has been calibrated against a real site.
- The practice data's weather is the actual (made-up) weather, so the forecast assumes a perfect weather forecast. In the app, live Open-Meteo weather is applied to the practice site.
- The detector was used as is. It was not trained or checked on the sample clip. Overhead views, night, rain and cars hidden behind each other cause missed or double counts. A 30-second clip gives only a rough hourly rate.
- The planner works greedily, shift by shift. It ignores breaks and minimum shift lengths, and it assumes extra crew scale linearly.

## Run it yourself

```bash
python tests/test_trafficflow.py           # simulator, forecast, planner and page API (standard library only)
pip install -r requirements.txt
python tests/test_vision.py                # car counter on the sample clip
python training/make_data.py               # regenerate the synthetic data (deterministic)
python training/train.py                   # retrain and export web/data/models.json
python training/evaluate_planner.py        # re-run the 14-day planner test (about 3 minutes)
sh build_site.sh && python -m http.server -d _site 8000   # then open http://localhost:8000
```

## Files

- `trafficflow/`: the Python package that runs in the browser and in the tests (`sim`, `forecast`, `planner`, `vision`, `api`)
- `training/`: data generator, model training and planner evaluation (scikit-learn, desktop only)
- `data/synthetic_drive_through.csv`: one row per 15-minute slot, 06:00–23:00, from 6 July to 11 October 2026 (synthetic). It is a plain CSV with no preamble.
- `web/`: the page (HTML, CSS, vanilla JavaScript, hand-written SVG charts) and the Pyodide runtime (0.29.5, with the NumPy and OpenCV wheels)
- `models/`: NanoDet-Plus ONNX model

## Built from

The app combines ideas from GeeksforGeeks Python projects, rebuilt for the browser: [Vehicle Detection in a Video frame](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/opencv-python-program-vehicle-detection-video-frame/) and [Scraping Weather Data](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/python/scraping-weather-data-using-python-to-get-umbrella-reminder-on-email/), together with queue simulation and scikit-learn regression. It continues an earlier JavaScript prototype (FlowLab), which estimated average queue size with a fluid model. This version adds per-vehicle simulation, finite waiting spaces and blocking, real waiting times, ML forecasting and staffing search. The tests check that it reproduces FlowLab's simple analytic cases.

## Fast loading

The page shows straight away and Python starts in the background, so nothing waits on a
"Starting Python" screen. Anything that needs Python simply waits for it if you are quicker.

- **Saved on the device** (`web/sw.js`): after the first visit, Python (Pyodide) and its packages are kept in
  the browser, so a refresh or a later visit doesn't download them again. Every app on the same site shares
  that one saved copy. The app's own files are still fetched fresh, so updates show up right away.
- **Quick restart** (`web/pyfast.js`): the first visit also saves a snapshot of a freshly started Python.
  Later visits restore it instead of starting Python from scratch (about 1.2 s down to 0.3 s on a laptop).
  If the snapshot can't be used (for example after a Pyodide upgrade), Python starts normally and a new one
  is saved.
- `build_site.sh` writes the Pyodide version into `sw.js`, so upgrading `web/pyodide/` needs no other change.
